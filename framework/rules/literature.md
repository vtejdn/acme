# Literature Rules

All fixed L reading notes and mathematical supplements belong in mathematics/ledger/, using the single author/line L sequence.
Source audits contain author-owned rolling U and source navigation, reusing adequate C/U/L rather than duplicating inspections.
Bibliographic identity belongs to references.bib; concise useful edition/source explanations belong to the source-specific README, and inspected mathematics and limits belong to U or an adequate existing responsible material.
Apply the [agent/tool responsibility](workflow.md#agent-and-tool-responsibilities) to identity, editions, mathematical applicability and necessary acquisition.
Literature preparation and source audits exclude associated Lean code unless its audit is explicitly requested under the [default audit scope](literature-audits.md#default-audit-scope).

<a id="source-preparation"></a>
## Common source preparation

Reuse sufficient existing mathematical and source evidence before obtaining or inspecting an original.
Use shared bibliography, edition-specific contents, concise source notes and adequate C/U/L at their actual scope.
When further source reading is necessary, reuse an applicable local original, look for relevant local candidates and then perform necessary authorized external search or acquisition.
The current question, work, edition and available facts determine the scope; stop once adequate material is available rather than scanning the whole store.
A candidate title, filename or identifier match is a lead, not mathematical identity, edition agreement or sufficient evidence.
Reading candidate metadata or passages requires no prior key registration, U creation, source ledger or search receipt.

Establish the real work and edition, maintain necessary bibliographic fields and useful source explanations, and inspect the actual passages required for use.
Process complete definitions, terminology and symbols through [def registration](definitions.md), preserving source conventions and application limits.
Reuse adequate inspection without a full-paper audit merely for a bibliographic or stylistic use.
Prepare or reuse [searchable contents](#searchable-contents) when the selected source actually supplies a contents list.
Use [progressive reading](#progressive-reading) and the [source-audit rules](literature-audits.md) at the needed depth.

<a id="bibliography-content"></a>
## Bibliographic content

Canonical and manuscript-subset BibTeX contain authors, title, publication/edition identity, venue/publisher, dates, pages/article numbers, identifiers and legitimate public source URLs.
A concise note may explain publication, language, edition uncertainty or a meaningful bibliographic exception.
It is not an acquisition log, local path/configuration, proof verdict, Task account or execution report.
Keep true publication fields, stable keys and uncertainty; remove duplicate workflow prose only after necessary unique facts have their responsible home.
Source notes and inspected mathematics retain their separate purposes, and metadata copying grants no inspection or redistribution authority.

<a id="local-pdf-storage"></a>
## Local Source Storage

Original PDFs, HTML and selected related resources use the Git-ignored logical entrance mathematics/literature/pdf/.
PDFs retain flat filenames; HTML and related resources use a directory named by the established work key.
The entrance is an ordinary directory or one top-level directory junction; symbolic links and nested linked paths are unsupported.
Original files stay local and outside Git/GitHub, framework copying and authored-material intake, even when publicly available elsewhere.
Keep relative logical links; necessary physical targets, access parameters and mount arrangements belong only to the responsible human's ignored [private setup](organization.md#machine-local-locators).
Each human is responsible for cross-machine validity of their links, with agent assistance within actual authority and no framework path synchronization or availability certification.

Concrete source access and file maintenance resolve the actual selected mount and verify containment, file shape and applicable access.
On first authorized retention, create the ordinary ignored entrance on demand if it is absent and no applicable external arrangement exists; clean installation and bibliography-only work need no directory or placeholder.
Preserve and report a broken junction, occupied non-directory entrance or inaccessible configured target instead of silently substituting another store.
Resolve only selected source operations; their authority does not extend to external sibling directories.
General scans, publication snapshots, copies and cleanup skip the entire mount before descent.
Never recursively delete or move through a junction as workspace cleanup.

Public links into the logical source root, groups or named files check normalization, containment and protected paths without resolving the mount or querying existence, bytes, format or native anchors.
After the existing single URL decoding, reject Windows trailing-dot/space aliases of source prefixes and necessary ancestors before target queries; protected components remain prohibited even inside canonical source paths.
Ordinary preparation and publication do not resolve an unselected current or retired source root and create no source-availability guard.
Startup, U read/check/show/discovery, navigation, ordinary maintenance and Git delivery do not check local missing, unregistered or fingerprint state.
Source absence neither invalidates sufficient shared U nor creates download, audit or registration debt.

There is no literature registry, Artifact/Resource account, holding ledger, routine inventory or literature-file fingerprint mechanism.
Do not calculate, retain, compare, display or propagate literature fingerprints, including historical accidental-damage baselines; do not relocate them into U, README or private files.
For an actually unreadable, suspicious, wrong-edition or damaged source, the agent and human choose adequate reading, reacquisition or direct comparison from the concrete facts.
Native Git identifiers and necessary temporary byte comparisons, expected-content checks, locks, occupancy and rollback retain their operation-local purpose without a persistent digest.

PDF names start with an ASCII letter or digit and otherwise use letters, digits, underscores, hyphens, periods and literal ASCII plus.
Use actual flat PDF filenames such as SourceKey.pdf or SourceKey--arxiv-v2.pdf; grouped PDFs remain unsupported.
Selected HTML and related resources use contained work-key groups.
Nested links, escapes, cross-work substitution and source-store publication as C/T/J material remain errors.
A concrete selected access failure is reported truthfully without inferring availability of another source or performing a routine whole-library check.

## Literature Acquisition

Within authorized research or source preparation, reuse an adequate local original before obtaining a missing selected work/edition.

Apply the [ACME material boundary](intake.md#scope-and-routing) to the actual selected content; external literature does not acquire ACME authorship or review status from its hosting platform.
Use bounded requests with timeouts and finite attempts, and preserve actual destination conflicts, containment and necessary format checks.
Retrieval tools use supplied addresses and report actual requested/final URLs and bytes; they do not choose work identity, canonical editions or fallback sources.
Inspect a response's intended format, readable content, title, authors and edition before using it as the selected original.
HTTP success or a filename suffix does not establish that a response is the intended paper.
Keep useful original locators and edition/use restrictions in concise author source notes; no registry, online exception form, fingerprint, date entry or completion receipt is required.

On actual failure, directly tell the human the work, inspected edition, known address, reason and resulting use limitation.
Retain a continuing useful limitation in the corresponding author's source notes or actual consumer, without a failure queue, periodic retry or routine warning from missing files.
Useful U may still be retained honestly; acquisition failure establishes neither invalid mathematics nor completed original preparation.
Continue authorized work independent of the missing source, without inventing access, retention, inspection or completion.
A same-work revision retains the citation key while exact original locators distinguish editions.
Preserve originals still needed by existing evidence before replacing a current link, and compare affected uses rather than silently assigning a newer edition to old checks.

Ordinary U reading, source navigation and Git delivery are not another import or source-acquisition event.

## Searchable tables of contents

When preparing a selected book, monograph or other source with an actual supplied contents list, reuse an adequate same-edition transcription or prepare a searchable UTF-8 Markdown transcription under mathematics/literature/contents/.
This includes user-supplied future material; a successful download alone does not complete the agent's contents preparation.
A source without a supplied contents list requires no invented file, page-count test or reconstructed list of body headings.
For PDF, use the complete source stem: pdf/SourceKey--edition.pdf corresponds to contents/SourceKey--edition.md.
For HTML, mirror the complete path below pdf/ and append .md, preserving native extensions and nested paths, such as contents/Work/edition-2/toc.htm.md.
Choose the actual page carrying the list; one transcription may cover several supplied pages with exact links and stated coverage.

Identify the real work, language, edition/volume, exact source and coverage, linking its logical original or verified same-edition primary contents excerpt.
For PDF, distinguish one-based contents-page positions from printed destination labels; do not assume a constant page offset.
For HTML, use actual chapter/section labels, native anchors and page paths, retaining genuine printed labels and omitting inapplicable PDF fields.
Transcribe the complete supplied hierarchy, titles, mathematical notation, appendices, unnumbered entries, Roman numerals and continuation pages in the original language.
Compare extracted/OCR text with actual pages, checking order, columns, wraps, numbering, symbols and labels; inspect images for scans or damaged text layers.
Keep extraction and page images disposable under the [temporary-storage rule](organization.md#temporary-storage).
Different editions/volumes retain distinct associations; an adequate same-edition list can be reused only after comparing actual lists and coverage.
A complete contents excerpt may coexist with only partial body access; state both limits without a holding account or source-file inventory.

Maintain the exact source links and affected consumers together.
The literature entrance links the generic contents guide and bibliography, rather than a second book list or per-source registration.
For a known work, use shared bibliography, source notes and inspected U/L before further original reading.
For a topic, search actual retained titles, for example `rg -n -i --glob '*.md' -- "Hankel|Toeplitz|Hardy" mathematics/literature/contents`, including relevant source-language terms.
Confirm edition/coverage and inspect the actual passage when needed; a hit is navigation, and no hit, absent transcription or unavailable source proves no result absence.
Use source indexes/full text and [progressive reading](#progressive-reading) as needed, without a Task-startup full contents scan.
Report unreadable entries, missing pages or unfinished transcription specifically in the normal handoff and useful responsible source notes.
Do not publish an empty placeholder, redirect another edition's list or create U/L/R merely for contents, extraction or a reading receipt.
Contents establish no mathematical review.
Clean installations include only the generic guide, with no named transcriptions or original files.

<a id=progressive-reading></a>

## Progressive Reading and Audit Triggers

These rules cover monographs, papers, and unpublished manuscripts.
Adding a source does not assign a full audit.
During actual source preparation, establish the work, edition, publication status, access restrictions and intended use from the source facts.
Keep useful edition and access limitations in concise author-owned source notes, without mandatory fields, acquisition dates or an exception account.
Inspect the title page and, as useful, the abstract, contents, index, or preface; label subject tags as provisional.
User-supplied sources may be retained for future research without an active goal, an empty U or a source-note entry.
Do not invent a research objective or claim that unread chapters have been reviewed.

For each research task, follow this sequence.
The root [reuse-before-proof rule](knowledge-reuse.md#reuse-before-proof) also triggers it before substantial work on a nontrivial intermediate claim: inspect existing knowledge, then relevant unaudited local sources, and then external primary sources when local evidence remains insufficient.
Stop advancing when enough evidence is available; do not wait until a proof attempt stalls or repeat an adequate unchanged search.

1. State the question and identify the definition, result, method, or decision that needs evidence.
   Reuse relevant existing audit units and source notes, checking their inspected editions and actual unchecked dependencies.
2. If reviewed material is insufficient, search unaudited local sources: use the [searchable contents route](#searchable-contents) and source indexes, then relevant terminology, equivalent formulations, theorem names, and citation leads.
   Trigger this step for a missing input, unclear convention, new mathematical lead, suspected conflict, or changed source version.
   Do not wait for a whole-source audit.
3. Read a promising passage with enough surrounding material to resolve notation and hypotheses.
   Record the exact source version, chapter/section, theorem or equation number, and printed/PDF page mapping where available.
   A search hit or extracted snippet is only a locator, not a verified result.
   If extraction is unreliable, inspect the relevant rendered pages; failed text search is not evidence of absence.
4. Before using a result in a proof, formalization, or substantive research conclusion, verify its exact source statement, version, locator, hypotheses, and local applicability.
   An ordinary established external theorem may be accepted without re-auditing its proof when no unresolved objection affects its use; document that acceptance and the actual inspection depth under the [accepted external-input rule](literature-audits.md#accepted-external-inputs).
   Trigger detailed proof review for a suspected error, a critical nonstandard input, incompatible conventions or a local extension requiring new reasoning, a version discrepancy, or an explicit task.
   Candidate routes may cite unaudited leads if clearly marked as conditional.
   Acceptance is not proof-assistant verification.
5. Expand to the definitions, lemmas, and dependency interfaces required by the current result.
   Distinguish accepted external inputs from unresolved evidence, applicability, or local deductions.
   An accepted foundational theorem does not become an unresolved condition merely because its proof was not reread; genuinely unresolved inputs and adaptations keep the affected conclusions conditional.
   Do not recursively audit every bibliography entry.
   Extend to an entire chapter or book only when the needed dependency structure or explicit task warrants it, recording the reason.
6. Stop when evidence is sufficient for the current decision, or record the exact unresolved gap and next useful action when access or repeated unproductive searches prevent progress.
   Reopen for new evidence, changed assumptions, or a new version; do not repeat unchanged searches by default.

Author-owned source notes identify useful editions and locators; U or adequate C/L owns actual inspected passages, mathematics and review limits.
Concrete cross-repository acquisition operations and their obtained or unavailable provenance belong in `intake/<initiator>/` under the [intake rule](intake.md#scope-and-routing).
Source-specific reading/search notes and theorem-level audit coverage belong in `mathematics/audits/literature/`; use a compact section of an existing source record until a reusable unit warrants its own record.
Do not create one file or unit for every search hit.
Describe actual inspection depth and unresolved limits in ordinary prose under the [source-audit rules](literature-audits.md#coverage-states), without an enumerated coverage state.
Existing full-source audits retain their documented scope; this policy neither downgrades them nor extends them to related sources.

## Unpublished and Restricted Material

Distinguish in the responsible source explanation the actual source type (for example, paper, monograph, or lecture notes), publication status (`published`, `preprint`, `draft`, or `unknown`), and access classification (`public`, `restricted`, or `unknown`).
Apply those facts to the exact source edition; no fixed field table is required.
A preprint whose public release has been verified follows the public-literature rules even if it has not appeared in a journal.
Private revisions, annotations, and accompanying communications do not inherit that public classification.

Private unpublished drafts default to local research use; existing explicit authorization governs any permitted disclosure.
Without authorization covering the action, do not upload restricted files or extracts to external services, publish or push them to a remote repository, or include private author information, nonpublic claims, or quotations in online searches.
This also applies to revealing audit notes and derived search queries.
Generic public mathematical terminology may be searched when it does not disclose private content.
If access classification is unknown, establish it before external disclosure.
General permission for public literature searches does not authorize disclosure of private material.
Material subject to copyright or distribution restrictions, especially original literature PDFs, stays outside Git tracking, uploads and outgoing material transfers.
The local source store's original PDF, HTML and resource files remain excluded even when publicly reachable; public availability alone selects no redistribution.
Relocating originals into J, attachments, another directory or large copied passages does not make them transferable agent-authored material.
The agent judges actual content and permitted disclosure, including revealing audit prose; scripts enforce known excluded paths and explicit publication boundaries without a copyright classifier.

## 1. Citation Keys and Filenames

Use readable, simplified alpha-style ASCII addresses, omitting diacritics from the key.
Exact reproduction of standard BibTeX alpha labels or typography is not required.
When an actual naming misuse is discovered, confirm the work and original naming basis and correct the key through coordinated maintenance, even if the incorrect address has been used consistently.
For the stated ordinary single-author Smith work with year basis 2026, use Smi26 and the applicable existing collision suffix, rather than Smith26.
Preserve legitimate existing allocations and original year bases; later publication metadata alone does not invalidate a correct earlier address.
Judge misuse from actual source facts rather than key length or a regenerated current-metadata label, without a startup inventory or automatic whole-library normalization.
Preserve the complete ordered author list and actual name/diacritic forms in references.bib.
Identify surname components and source order correctly: ordinary single surnames use their three-character abbreviation; two through four authors contribute their surname initials; five or more contribute the first three names plus literal + before the year.
Retain the supported short/compound surnames, particles and editor/organization fallbacks.
Do not shorten author fields with and others to manufacture a key.
Use an established Latin transliteration only for the portable label and diagnose collisions.

Work identity uses stable source identifiers and actual bibliographic authorship, not the shortened key.
Compare supported BibTeX personal-name forms by their components and author order, interpreting equivalent TeX/Unicode name characters without rewriting the original fields or evaluating arbitrary TeX.
For example, `Dra\v{z}en` and `Dražen` identify the same spelling; an accent-free key is not evidence that two different full names identify the same author.
No initials expansion, fuzzy person matching or person registry is implied.

At initial registration, use formal publication year when known, otherwise first public release, otherwise the known private draft year with its basis.
A genuinely unknown identity/year needs an explicitly grounded provisional address; do not invent authorship or a publication date.
Later publication metadata does not recalculate an established address.

Primary PDFs use mathematics/literature/pdf/<key>.pdf; additional versions use <key>--<version>.pdf.
Original HTML pages/resources use the established `<key>/` group with explicit edition/page locators.
Source U directories, local explicit anchors, citations and writing choices use the same stable key.
Native HTML anchors and original source text are opaque source content, preserved during local key/directory maintenance.
Plus is a literal key character, not a form-encoded space; quote it as %2B in URLs and escape it in regular expressions.
It does not expand the human/repository name syntax.

## 2. Collisions, Versions, and Key Stability

Compare the full collection case-insensitively.
An unused base receives no suffix.
Once occupied, assign only the new distinct works b, c and onward; never rename the first registered work to add a.
Keep existing addresses stable, retain the established suffix progression beyond z and do not fill earlier holes, reorder the group or regenerate keys from a manuscript subset.
Identify same-work acquisitions or editions before resolving a shortened-key collision; reuse their established key and keep exact inspected editions distinct in source notes and the actual U inspection scope.

## 5. Checklist for Adding or Updating Literature

1. Identify the actual expected use and work/edition, reusing sufficient evidence and applicable local originals before necessary acquisition.
2. Establish true bibliography and access facts, check the selected source's intended format/content, and respect disclosure restrictions.
3. Maintain the stable canonical key and necessary bibliographic fields; retain only useful author-specific edition/source notes and real use limitations.
4. Complete necessary original preparation to reuse, acquisition or a truthful failed result, reporting failure directly to the human without a ledger or receipt.
5. Keep actual inspected mathematics and limits in U or adequate C/L, with complete definitions and applicable def registration.
6. Reuse or prepare actual same-edition contents when supplied, reporting unfinished work accurately.
7. Repair affected source notes, U, contents and downstream references, preserving actual objects, hypotheses, adoption and review scope under [shared maintenance](records.md#substantive-material-retention).

Preparatory originals and their quantity create no registration, cleanup, download or audit debt.
Ordinary startup and shared-material operations do not inventory their local availability.
